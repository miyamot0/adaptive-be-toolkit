# Debugging and Visualizations

## Beliefs Distribution

The **Beliefs Distribution** figure provides insights into the algorithm's belief distribution over levels (e.g., delays or prices) based on participant responses. This distribution is updated using a Bayesian approach, where each response shifts the probability mass towards the estimated target region.

### Certainty and ED50/OMAX/PMAX

Certainty in beliefs is represented by the height of the belief distribution at different levels. The **ED50** (Discounting) or **OMAX/PMAX** (Demand) values are key points where the algorithm converges, indicating the most likely expenditure region. Higher certainty around these values suggests that additional trials are unlikely to provide new information.

### Belief Update (Bayesian Update)

The algorithm updates its beliefs using a Bayesian approach, where each response shifts the probability mass towards the estimated target region. This update process is crucial for selecting the next question and refining the estimate of **ED50** or **OMAX/PMAX**.

## Entropy and Average Entropy Change

The **Entropy** figure provides insights into the uncertainty of beliefs across levels. Shannon entropy measures concentration of information and provides a metric for the amount of information for us to feel some level of 'confidence' that no further exploration would provide more precise estimates. The **Average Entropy Change** figure tracks how entropy changes across trials, indicating how much new information is being gained with each response (i.e., less change happening = less to gain from further queries).

### Dynamic Stop Point

Changes in entropy can indicate when additional trials are unlikely to contribute new information. A plateau in entropy suggests that the algorithm has converged and is likely at or near the optimal solution. This helps in determining a dynamic 'stop point' for the task. As a general baseline rule, a value of around 0.1 bits of average entropy change (nats) is a reasonable threshold for considering termination, but this can be adjusted based on the specific research context and desired precision.
